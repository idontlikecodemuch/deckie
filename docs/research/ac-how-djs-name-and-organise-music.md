# Research AC: how DJs name and organise their music

Dated Nov 14, 2026. Web research and reading only: no code, no build, no measurement, no commit. The brief is the
owner's reaction, the same day, to the eight suggestions at the end of letter AA2 (the lookup tried on a real
library): "all of this needs to be couched in how DJs use and organize their music", and, on one of them, that a DJ
would use the version or "dirty" as part of the song's title. This letter builds on AA2, on letter AA (section 6,
versions), on letters C and E and the community letter, on `docs/product/voice.md`, and on decisions D19 (versions
stay separate rows), D24, D26, D27, D30, D34 and D36. It does not repeat them.

Tags: [read] = I opened the page today and the finding is from it; [snip] = seen only through a search summary, the
page itself did not open; [AA], [AA2], [E] = taken from that letter, not re-opened; [inf] = my inference, with the
reasoning; [use] = common knowledge of DJ practice that I could not pin to a page today; [unverified] = I could not
open or confirm it. Source numbers are AC1, AC2 and so on, listed at the end. Sources are paraphrased; the words in
quotation marks are labels and terms as DJs and services write them. Every file name used as an example is invented
(Marlo Dune, Kaya Venn, Tessa Ray and DJ Ostro are not real artists).

## In plain words

A **version** is one particular cut of a song: the album cut, a longer cut for clubs, a remix by someone else, a cut
with the swearing taken out. A **record pool** is a subscription service that sends DJs new music, usually several
versions of each song, some of them made by the pool's own editors. An **edit** is a version made by cutting and
rearranging an existing recording rather than producing new music. **Clean** means the swearing is removed or
covered; **dirty** (or **explicit**) means it is left in. An **intro edit** is a pool edit that puts a few bars of
plain drums before the song starts, so a DJ has something to mix on. A **credit** is how the artists are written
("Marlo Dune feat. Kaya Venn"). A **compilation** is a release that gathers songs by many artists.

The short answers:

- **To a DJ, the version is part of the song's name.** Every guide for DJs, every pool and every DJ store writes a
  song as artist, title, then the version in brackets, and tells DJs to keep it that way, because two versions of
  one song are two different tools. The owner's reading is the DJ's reading. A catalogue's reading (the version is a
  note on a recording) is the odd one out.
- **"Clean" and "dirty" are a fact about the song, and DJs filter on it.** DJs keep separate crates, separate
  sticks, a colour or a word in a tag so that nothing with swearing is played at a wedding or a children's party.
  No DJ program has a proper field for it, and DJs have asked for one. Deckie should keep it as its own fact and
  let the booth show it and filter on it.
- **Pool words describe what was done to the recording and what the DJ can do with it.** "Intro", "Short Edit",
  "Quick Hit", "Acap In", "Transition 100-128", "Redrum": each tells the DJ how the file starts, how long it runs
  and how it mixes. None of them is in any catalogue. They must never be thrown away from the song; they should
  only be left out of the few words sent to a lookup.
- **A DJ's folders are the DJ's own filing, and they come in four kinds**: an artist's album, a compilation, a
  dated folder of downloads, and a category the DJ made ("Wedding", "90s", "Warm up"). Only the first says who the
  artist is.
- **The first credited artist is the one a DJ files a song under**, and any name in the credit is one a DJ may
  type when searching. The many ways of joining names ("feat.", "ft.", "&", "x", "vs", commas) are spelling, not
  meaning.
- **For a song from a compilation a DJ wants two different things**: where their copy is (the compilation, the
  disc, the folder) and when the song is from (the year it first came out). These are two facts, and neither should
  replace the other.
- **The eight suggestions mostly stand, reworded.** The main change: stop speaking of words being "dropped". Three
  things are kept apart: what is **kept** on the song (everything, as the DJ wrote it), what is **shown** (the title
  with its version, the way DJs write it), and what is **sent** to a lookup (the bare title and the first artist).
  Section 5 goes through the eight one by one and ends with a proposed vocabulary of version kinds.

## 1. What a song's name is to a DJ

### 1.1 The name has three parts, and the third is not optional

| Who says so | What they say | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| A DJ school's book chapter on preparing music | Rename every file to artist, then title, then the remix or version in brackets, and put the version in brackets inside the title tag too; its worked example is a well-known house record written with its club mix in the name | [read] AC34 |
| A DJ software maker's own article on library clean-up (Sep 2026) | A file name should show artist, title, mix version and whether it is clean or explicit, so the DJ is not guessing mid-set | [read] AC36 |
| A DJ glossary's page on file naming | Put the mix name in the file name, in brackets: a name that cannot tell two versions apart means the wrong one gets loaded | [read] AC13 |
| A DJ magazine's plea to producers (Aug 2015) | Title is "Title (Remixer Name Remix)", or Bootleg, or Edit; artist is the original artist | [read] AC35 |
| DJ stores | Beatport carries a separate mix name on every song and asks labels to say "Extended Mix" there so that DJs know | [snip] AC25; [AA] |
| DJ programs | rekordbox has Mix Name and Remixer fields [AA]; VirtualDJ has a Remix field that it fills from the brackets of a file name of the form "Artist - Title (Remix)"; Serato lists Remixer among the fields a smart crate can match on | [read] AC52; [snip] AC40 |
| Record pools | The same song is listed several times, once per version, and the version and "Clean" or "Dirty" are in the file name and the title tag | [read] AC5; [read] AC51 |

So a DJ's name for a song is **artist, title, version**, and where it applies **clean or dirty**. A DJ searching
types any of these: the title, an artist, a remixer's name, "intro", "dirty". The owner's two examples are exactly
how a DJ would say them.

What catalogues do instead, for contrast:

| Catalogue | Version | Clean or dirty | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| MusicBrainz | In the title, in brackets, lower case; a remix or an edit is its own recording, a remaster is not | An edit made by censoring is an edit, so its own recording; no field or rule names "clean" | [read] AC20; [AA] |
| Apple's style guide for labels | In brackets after the title; "Album Version" and "Original Mix" are banned from titles | A flag on the song. The words "Explicit Version" and "Dirty" are banned from titles; "Clean" is flagged only where an explicit twin exists | [read] AC23 |
| Spotify's style guide | In a version field, not the title | A flag | [snip] AC24 |
| Beatport | A mix name on every song; "Original Mix" where none is given | Shown as a mark or in the mix name, depending on the program | [snip] AC25, AC57; [AA] |

The gap is plain. A pool writes "(Dirty)" where a store sets a flag, and writes "(Intro)" where no store has
anything at all.

Two things in that table matter most for Deckie.

1. **A version's words can stack.** "(DJ Ostro Remix) (Intro) (Clean)" is three facts: who remade it, how it
   starts, and whether it swears. Pool names often carry the pool or editor too: one seller's pattern is the
   artist, the title, then the editor's name and edit type in one bracket and "Clean" or "Dirty" in the next [read]
   AC9.
2. **"Radio" means two things.** In dance music it is the short one. In hip-hop it is the clean one. A distributor
   now tells dance labels not to call the short version "Radio Edit" at all, but "Original Mix", with the long one
   "Extended Mix" [read] AC15. So "Original Mix" can be the long cut on an older release and the short cut on a
   newer one. Only the length says which.

### 1.3 Who uses what

| Kind of DJ | The versions they live on | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Open format, mobile, wedding | Clean and dirty pairs, intro edits, short edits and quick hits, redrums of older songs, transitions, radio edits. They choose the version for the room: a pool's guide, a DJ software maker's article and DJs' own threads all tie clean to weddings, corporate work and children's parties | [read] AC5, AC36, AC51 |
| Club: house, techno, trance | Extended mixes and remixes; dubs; almost never clean or dirty words. The label and the remixer are part of how they know a record | [read] AC2, AC14, AC51 |
| Hip-hop | Dirty and clean, instrumentals and acapellas, acap-in and acap-out edits, party breaks, blends | [read] AC9, AC11; [E] |
| Turntablists | Instrumentals, acapellas, bonus beats, scratch tools, and two copies of one record ("doubles") | [snip] AC17, AC56; [E] |
| Drum and bass, bass music | VIPs, bootlegs, dubplates | [read] AC12 |
| CD DJs | Whatever the above burned to disc, plus remix-service CDs; the version is whatever the handwritten or printed tracklist says | [read] AC47; [snip] AC16 |
| Radio | Clean, and radio length. US broadcast rules bar indecent material from 6 in the morning to 10 at night | [snip] AC58 |

### 1.4 Which are the same recording

By D24's test (same length within a second, every three-second block the same), **only copies and remasters** are
the same recording as the original. Everything else in the table is different audio, and so its own row in Deckie,
as the note on D19 requires. That includes a clean edit: it has the original's length and arrangement, but the
blocks with covered words differ [inf, from AC5 and D24].

What differs is how far each is from the original, and this is what a lookup can use:

| Distance | Kinds | A catalogue entry for the original gives |
|---|---|---|
| The same recording | a copy, a remaster, the plain one written with other words | everything |
| The same take, same length | clean, dirty | artist, year, label, release; not the words "clean" or "dirty" |
| The same take, other length | radio length, long, intro, outro, short, acap in and out, redrum, DJ edit | artist, and the original's year and label, said as the original's |
| Another production | remix, dub, VIP, bootleg, mashup, transition, party tools, parts | the original artist; the original's year, said as the original's; often its own entry exists for an official remix or dub |
| Another performance | live, acoustic, demo, re-recorded | the artist only; it needs its own entry |

A DJ's mark is information the DJ put there on purpose. It is noise to a lookup and meaning to the DJ.

### 2.3 Several artists

| Where | How the credit is written | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| MusicBrainz | Featured artists go in the artist credit, never the title, joined as the release prints it ("feat.", "featuring", another language's word). With no printed join: commas, and "&" before the last. Each credited artist is a separate, known name inside the credit | [read] AC21 |
| Apple's style guide | The main performer is "Primary"; a featured artist has a "Featuring" or "With" role and is never Primary; one artist per field; "vs." and "Meets" artists are all Primary, and those words belong in the title; five or more Primary artists on an album become "Various Artists" | [read] AC23 |
| Spotify's style guide | Each featured artist is its own credit; no featured names in titles or version fields | [snip] AC24 |
| Discogs | The name as credited on the release, with a join field holding "Featuring", "And", "/", "," and the like | [snip] AC26 |
| Pools and DJ files | One artist tag holding the whole credit as text: "ft.", "feat", "Feat.", "featuring", "f.", "w/", "with", "&", "and", "x", "vs", "vs.", "+", "/", commas, "pres.", "presents", and Spanish "y" and "con" | [AA2]; [snip] AC38; [use] |
| The tag formats | The older MP3 tag joins several artists with "/", which collides with names that contain one; the newer one separates them invisibly; Picard writes the readable credit in the artist tag and the separate names in a second tag | [snip] AC27 |
| DJ guides | The artist tag should include the featured artists; decide once what to do with "The" | [read] AC34 |

Which is the main artist to a DJ [inf]: **the first one credited.** It is the name the file sorts under in any list
by artist, the one Apple's rules call Primary, and the one a guest or a request slip uses. But a DJ searching types
whatever they remember, and DJ programs search by any part of the text, so a featured rapper's name or a remixer's
name must find the song too. Two traps:

- **A remixer or editor in the artist's place.** Producers of bootlegs and edits often put their own name as the
  artist [read] AC35. The "artist" a file name gives may be the person in the version.
- **A name that contains a join word.** A duo with "&" in its name, a band with commas in its name, an artist with
  "x" or "/" in theirs. Splitting a DJ's credit on join words breaks these. The safe direction is the other one:
  the catalogue's credit is already split into names, so ask whether each of its names appears whole in the DJ's
  text [inf].

## 3. How DJs organise music

### 3.1 Folders on the drive

The advice from DJ schools is to keep the folders shallow and let the DJ program's playlists do the sorting,
because deep folders are slow to browse and break when files move [snip] AC45; [read] AC41. What DJs actually have
is a mix of four kinds of folder:

| Kind of folder | Looks like | What its name tells Deckie | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| An artist's album | `Marlo Dune - First Light (2019)`, or `Marlo Dune/First Light/` | The artist and the release | [AA2]; [snip] AC37 |
| A compilation | `Club Summer 2004`, `VA - Club Summer 2004`, `Compilations/...` | The release only. Never the artist | [AA2]; [read] AC23 |
| A dated or source folder | `2026-09`, `New`, `Inbox`, a pool's name, a month | When or where the DJ got it. Forum posters describe a folder per month of downloads and a "NEW" folder sorted weekly | [snip] AC45 |
| The DJ's own category | `Wedding`, `90s Hip Hop`, `Warm up`, `Peak`, `Acapellas`, `Tools`, a venue or a party's name | How the DJ plays it: genre, era, event, energy, kind of tool | [read] AC39, AC41, AC42 |

A stick written by rekordbox adds a fifth: a `Contents` folder with a folder per artist and per album inside it,
made by the program from the tags, beside a `PIONEER` folder that holds the library [snip] AC49. There the folder
names are the tags again, not the DJ's filing.

### 3.2 Crates and playlists in the DJ programs

| Program | Its groups | What else sorts | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| Serato | Crates and subcrates; smart crates that fill themselves by rules on artist, tempo, comment, composer, file name, genre, grouping, key, label, rating, remixer, title, year and more | Colours on songs; up to 20 columns; search as you type | [snip] AC40, AC59; [read] AC39 |
| rekordbox | Playlists and playlist folders; intelligent playlists by rule; My Tag, the DJ's own labels in a few groups | Colours, ratings, comments; Mix Name and Remixer columns | [read] AC41; [AA]; the manual (AC50) would not open as text, [unverified] |
| Traktor | Playlists and folders; a second comment field and a colour that only Traktor reads | Rating, written into the file | [snip] AC54 |
| Engine DJ | Playlists and crates; a short list of fields; no grouping, no rating written to files | Colours, which DJs use for energy and for explicit | [read] AC51 |
| VirtualDJ | The drive's own folders, shown as they are; virtual folders and filter folders by rule | A Remix field, two free fields, grouping, colour, rating | [read] AC52 |

How working DJs describe their own systems, from Serato's article on four open-format DJs and from DJ schools
[read] AC39, AC41, AC42, AC43:

- **By genre, then sub-genre**, with the genres chosen by the DJ.
- **By era**: decade crates, or smart crates on the year. Mobile DJs on Serato's forum describe a folder per
  decade with crates inside for cocktail hour, dancing and so on, and a crate of nothing but wedding music [snip]
  AC46.
- **By event**: a crate per residency or party, a wedding crate, a folder brought out for one season, a "holding"
  crate for finished sets, and the history of past sets kept as an archive.
- **By energy or time of night**: three tiers, or stars, or colours; "warm-up", "peak", "closer".
- **By kind of tool**: acapellas, samples, loops, scratch sounds kept apart from songs.
- **By search.** One of Serato's four says the search box is a large part of his organisation.

### 3.3 A stick for CDJs

With a rekordbox library on it, the player browses by **categories** (artist, album, playlist, key, history and
others) and sorts by items, and both lists are chosen by the DJ in rekordbox before the stick is written; playlists
show a running number [read] AC48; [snip] AC49. The DJ is meant to browse playlists, not the `Contents` folder.
Without a library, the player shows the stick's own folders and files as a tree, up to eight levels deep, with the
track names down the left edge [read] AC48; whether those names come from the tags or the file names is
[unverified], which is one more reason DJs keep both right. A player with no search and a small screen is where a
file name that reads "artist, title, version" matters most.

### 3.4 Burned CDs

From DJ forum threads, the fullest from January 2013 [read] AC47; others [snip]:

- **How many songs a disc**: from one song per disc, through 5 to 10, to full discs and MP3 discs of a hundred or
  more.
- **Every song burned twice**, on different discs, so that two songs from one disc can be mixed. A CD DJ's
  duplicates are deliberate.
- **Sorted by genre**, sometimes by key or by how reliable the songs are (a "no-brainer" disc); wallets by genre or
  by colour.
- **The names live on paper**: a printed or handwritten tracklist per disc with the track number, the artist, the
  title, the remix, the tempo and the key; the disc itself carries a number or a letter in marker pen, since paper
  labels unbalance and peel.
- **A wallet of favourites as the backup** when the laptop fails or the venue's players are old.

This fits what the community letter found and what letter AA built for: the disc's own name is a number or a short
word, and the song's name, with its version, is on a list beside it.

### 3.5 Compilations

To a store a compilation is a release with "Various Artists" as its artist [read] AC23. To Apple's Music app it is
a folder under `Compilations`, kept whole [snip] AC37. To a DJ it is a source of single songs: the DJ school's
chapter says to tag each song on its own because DJs pick songs, not albums, to strip the track numbers, and to
check the year, because a download's year is often the compilation's or the remaster's, not the song's [read] AC34.
So compilations sit in a DJ's library three ways: **kept whole** as a folder with numbered files (the trial library
is mostly this [AA2]); **split up** and filed by genre or era with the compilation surviving only in the album tag;
or **burned as a disc**, where the compilation is the disc. Two special cases: a song from a **DJ-mixed**
compilation is a different, shorter cut that starts inside its neighbour [read] AC22, and a **remix-service** disc
is a compilation of versions that exist nowhere else [snip] AC16.

### 3.6 Duplicates, and several versions of one song

- **Versions are kept on purpose.** VirtualDJ's own article: a radio edit and an extended mix are not duplicates
  to a DJ, they are different tools [read] AC36. The owner said the same on Nov 1 (note on D19).
- **Copies are kept on purpose too**, sometimes: two discs, two sticks, a better-quality copy beside an old one,
  the same song on a compilation and on the album, and doubles for juggling [read] AC47, AC51; [snip] AC56.
- **Clean and dirty are kept as pairs**, and told apart by a word in the title, a word in grouping, a colour, a
  separate crate tree or a separate stick [read] AC51, AC52.
- **Accidental copies** (the same download twice) are the only duplicates DJs want gone [read] AC36.

## 5. What this means for Deckie

All of this section is [inf] unless tagged. It decides nothing.

### 5.1 Three things, kept apart

The suggestions in AA2 spoke of words being "dropped". That one word is what made them read as a catalogue's view.
For a DJ's song there are three separate things:

| | What it is | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Kept** | Everything the file and the DJ say: the title, the whole artist credit, the whole version text, clean or dirty, the DJ's mark, the folder, the file name | Nothing is ever removed or rewritten by Desk or by a lookup (D34, and AA 6.4: never let an answer remove a bracket) |
| **Shown** | The title with its version in brackets, the way DJs write it; the credit as the DJ wrote it; clean or dirty as a small word | The version is on the booth's row, because it is how two rows of one song are told apart (note on D19) |
| **Sent** | The few words a lookup needs: the bare title and the first credited artist | Version words, clean and dirty, pool and editor names, the DJ's mark, a key or tempo in the name, a website's name and track numbers are left out of what is sent. They are used afterwards, to judge the candidates |

An example. The file `X Marlo Dune ft. Kaya Venn - Night Drive (DJ Ostro Intro - Dirty).mp3`:

- **Kept**: mark "X"; credit "Marlo Dune ft. Kaya Venn"; title "Night Drive"; version "DJ Ostro Intro - Dirty".
- **Read from the version** (section 5.5): kind intro; by DJ Ostro; lyrics dirty.
- **Shown**: "Night Drive (DJ Ostro Intro - Dirty)", Marlo Dune ft. Kaya Venn, with "dirty" as its word.
- **Sent**: title "Night Drive", artist "Marlo Dune".
- **Expected back**: the original, some seconds shorter than the file, since an intro was added. Its year and label
  are offered as the original's. Its title never replaces the DJ's; "clean" or "dirty" is never taken from it.

### 5.2 Clean or dirty as a fact of its own

Yes. The evidence: pools and DJs write it on nearly every hip-hop, pop and Latin file [read] AC5, AC9; stores
make it a flag, not a word [read] AC23; DJs separate their whole library by it for some gigs and have asked their
programs for a field [read] AC51, AC52; none has one. It is also the one version word that is not really about the
version: it combines with any of them.

A proposal for its shape:

- **Three values: clean, dirty, not said.** "Not said" is never shown as clean. Most songs with nothing said have
  no swearing, but Deckie cannot know which.
- **Filled from the name and tags** (the words in section 5.5, in the title, the version, grouping or comment), or
  **set by the DJ in Desk**. Never from a lookup's candidate, because the catalogue entry that fits by length may
  be the other twin. Never guessed from the sound.
- **"Radio Edit" is a hint, not an answer**: it usually means clean in hip-hop and only short in dance music.
- **An instrumental has no words**, so it can count as safe for a clean filter.
- **In the booth**: a small word on the row where it is said, and a filter on the picks. The simple form of the
  filter leaves out every song said to be dirty. A stricter form (only songs said to be clean, plus instrumentals)
  would suit a school dance and would hide most of an older crate. Which form, or both, is the owner's call.
- **It travels as an addition to the crate file**, like the other facts in D34.

### 5.3 The eight suggestions, one by one

**1. The artist from the folder.** Right for one kind of folder and wrong for three (section 3.1). What would
break: a compilation's folder, a month folder or "Wedding" becoming the artist of every song in it, shown in the
booth and sent to a lookup. The DJ-first version:

- Take the artist from the folder only when everything agrees: the files have no artist of their own and are
  numbered; the folder, or the folder above it, reads as an artist and an album; no tag in the folder names
  another artist; and nothing says compilation (the tag's compilation mark, "Various", "VA", a `Compilations`
  folder, files whose tags name different artists).
- Say where it came from ("from the folder"), as every fact does under D34, so a DJ can see it is a reading.
- For a **compilation** folder the folder's name is the **release**. That is worth more than an artist: one lookup
  of the release returns every song on it with its artist, as a disc lookup does (letter AA), and a tracklist file
  in the folder does the same with nothing leaving.
- For a **dated folder** or a **DJ's own category**, take nothing for the name. The folder stays what D26 already
  makes it: the song's place.

**2. A version after a second dash, and "clean", "dirty", "explicit" and website names.** Reading the part after a
second dash as the version is right and is how DJs write it. "Drop" was the wrong word. DJ-first:

- The part after the second dash is **kept as the song's version** and shown in brackets like any other.
- "Clean", "Dirty" and "Explicit" are **kept in the version as written, and also read into the lyrics fact**.
- Pool and edit words (section 5.5) are kept the same way. No catalogue holds them, so a lookup for such a song
  is a lookup for **the original**, and should say so.
- Only **what is sent** leaves them out. The same goes for a website's name, a key or tempo, a track number and
  the DJ's mark.
- A website's name or a working note ("MASTER2", "Official Video", "Free Download") is the one thing a DJ would
  want cleaned off the shown name. Offer it as a batch change with a preview, like Desk's other naming tools; do
  not do it silently.
- The DJ's mark is kept and shown as the DJ's. Desk should not guess what it means.

**3. Asking once more with free words, and sending only the first of several artists.** Right, with two cautions
from how DJ files are credited:

- **Send the first credited artist**, and keep the whole credit. If nothing is found and the version names a
  person, try the title with that person, because a bootleg's "artist" is often its remixer (section 2.3).
- **A second ask must not lower the bar.** Free words find the song when the artist is cut or swapped, but they
  also find covers and tributes. What comes back should still need a whole-name match on the artist before Desk
  chooses by itself; otherwise it is a list.
- What would annoy: a second pass that doubles the wait without saying so. Say how many songs it is for.

**4. "The same title by the same first artist is one song."** Not in a DJ's world. One artist can have, under one
title: an album cut and a single cut, a clean and a dirty twin of the same length, a live take, an acoustic take,
a re-recording years later, a part one and a part two, and the catalogue often lists the same recording twice.
DJ-first:

- Count as **one name** only candidates that differ in **how the credit or title is written**: a featured artist
  in the credit or in the title's brackets, "feat." against "ft.", "&" against "and", commas, "The", "Pt." against
  "Part".
- **Never** fold across a version kind, across clean and explicit, or across years more than one apart. Those are
  different records to a DJ and stay a list.
- When several candidates fold to one name, **fill only what they agree on**. If their years or labels differ,
  those stay unticked.
- A cover, a tribute or a karaoke copy of the same length has another artist, so the whole-name rule in
  suggestion 6 already keeps it out.
- As AA2 says, the trial's rows do not keep the other candidates, so this needs a look at real rows first.

**5. Comparing versions by kind.** Right, and section 5.5 is the table for it. The rules a DJ would expect:

- **Spelling is not a difference.** "Extended", "Extended Mix", "Extended Version" and "12" Mix" are one kind.
  "Rmx" is "Remix". "Original Mix" is no version.
- **A named person must match.** Two remixes are the same only if the same name is in both.
- **Length settles the rest** (AA 6.4.3). A plain name against a "radio edit" or "album version" of the same
  length is the same record: choose it. The DJ's song keeps having no version; the catalogue's word is offered
  unticked, since "radio edit" tells a DJ something useful (little intro) but is the catalogue's word, not theirs.
- **Clean or dirty with a fitting length** is the same take: choose it for artist, year, label and release, and
  leave the lyrics fact alone. This is a real gain for pool files: 30 songs in the trial had these words and 7
  percent were chosen [AA2].
- **A pool kind never equals a catalogue kind.** "Intro", "Short Edit", "Quick Hit", "Acap In", "Redrum" and
  "Transition" mean the candidate is the original. Its length will not fit, and should not be expected to. Whether
  Desk may then choose the original for its year and label, marked as the original's, when exactly one fits by
  title and whole artist name, is a loosening of today's rule and is the owner's call after a look at the rows.
- **Brackets that are not a version** must not be read as one: part of the title, a featured credit, "from" a
  film, a year, a label, a website (the list is in 5.5).

**6. "Agrees" by whole words, not "contains".** Right as written, and nothing in DJ practice argues against it.
Add: compare artists **by credited names** (each of the catalogue's names found whole in the DJ's credit), and
compare titles after the version and any featured credit are taken out of both. DJs' titles are often one short
word, and a one-word title inside a longer one is the wrong song.

**7. Which release a compilation song belongs to.** Both, as two facts, because a DJ asks two questions.

- **"Where is it?"** The copy's own release: the compilation, the disc, the folder. This is the file's album tag
  and Deckie's place (D24, D26, D27). A DJ finds the record by it. A lookup must never replace it.
- **"When is it from?"** The year the song first came out, and the label it came out on. This is what a DJ sorts
  eras by, and it is exactly what compilation downloads get wrong [read] AC34; [AA2]. A lookup is good at it.
- So the lookup's first own release is right to fetch, but it fills a **first-out year and label**, shown as
  such, and leaves the file's release alone. Where the file has a year that is the compilation's, both are kept
  and the first-out year is the one to sort by.
- For a song with a version the catalogue did not have, the year is the original's and is said so (AA 6.4.4).
- Sending the file's release with the words, as Desk already does [AA2], stays useful: it helps find the right
  candidate even though the release is not what gets filled.

**8. The trial tool's compare.** Right, and it should go further, so that the next trial measures what a DJ would
call success: compare credits by names; compare versions by kind; count "found the original of a DJ's version" as
its own outcome, not as a miss or a wrong version; and count separately how often the song's own name was left
exactly as the DJ had it, which should be always.

### 5.4 The eight, rewritten

1. **Artist from the folder**: only from a folder that is plainly one artist's album, shown as read from the
   folder; a compilation's folder names the release and can be looked up whole; a dated folder or a DJ's own
   category names nothing but the place.
2. **Versions**: whatever follows the title, in brackets or after a second dash, is the song's version, kept and
   shown as written; clean or dirty is also kept as a fact of its own; only the words sent to a lookup leave these
   out, along with marks, keys, numbers and website names.
3. **A second ask**: when title and artist find nothing, ask once more with free words, sending the first credited
   artist, then the name in the version; never choose from it without a whole-name match.
4. **Several names fit**: fold candidates that differ only in how the credit or title is spelled; never fold
   across a version kind, clean against explicit, or years apart; fill only what the folded candidates agree on.
5. **Versions by kind**: compare kind, named person and length, not spelling; a same-length radio or album cut is
   the song; a same-length clean or dirty twin gives facts but not the lyrics fact; a pool edit's candidate is the
   original and is said to be.
6. **Agreement**: whole words and whole credited names; titles compared without versions and featured credits.
7. **Compilation songs**: the copy's release and place stay the file's; a lookup adds the year and label the song
   first came out with, as their own facts, and those are what eras sort by.
8. **The trial tool**: score credits by names and versions by kind, count "original found for a DJ's version"
   apart, and check that no DJ's name was changed.

### 5.5 A vocabulary of version kinds for Deckie, proposed

This is for three jobs: reading a name, comparing a song with a looked-up candidate, and showing versions. Desk's
lookup today knows nine marks (live, remix, edit, dub, instrumental, acapella, extended, other, mix) and a short
list of words that mean no version (`DeskLookup.swift`); this widens both and adds the pool's words. Matching
ignores case, accents, full stops, hyphens and the plural; "mix", "version", "edit" and "cut" after a kind word
add nothing ("Extended Mix" is "Extended").

**Reading a version.** Take every bracket group at the end of the title, and anything after a second dash. Split a
group on " - ", "/" and ",". In each piece find lyric words, then kind words; the words left over are a **name**
(a remixer, an editor or a pool). A song can have several kinds, one lyrics value and several names. The text
itself is always kept whole.

| Kind | Family | Words that map to it (with variants) | Different audio from the plain one? | Says clean or dirty? | Shown |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `plain` | Plain | original mix, original, original version, album version, album mix, LP version, LP, main, main mix, main version, single version, standard, full version, full length, stereo | No | "main" and "LP" lean dirty in hip-hop promos; treat as not said | As written if the DJ wrote it; never added |
| `remaster` | Plain | remaster, remastered, digital remaster, a year with either | No (MusicBrainz) | No | As written |

**The lyrics fact** (not a kind; it goes with any kind):

| Value | Words |
|---|---|
| clean | clean, clean edit, clean version, clean mix, radio clean, super clean, squeaky clean, edited, censored, amended, "#clean" |
| dirty | dirty, explicit, explicit version, uncensored, unedited, raw, street, "#dirty", "#explicit" |
| not said | anything else, including "radio", "main" and "LP" by themselves |

**Not a version.** Brackets holding any of these are not read as a kind: a featured credit ("feat.", "ft.", "with",
"prod."); a part of the title ("Part 2", "Pt. 2", "Reprise", "Interlude", "Skit", a phrase with no kind word);
"from" a film or show; a bare year; a label or catalogue number; a key code or a tempo; a bitrate; a website; "free
download", "official video", "official audio", "lyric video", "promo", "master", "final", "preview". The first
group is kept in the credit, the second in the title, and the rest are kept as written and offered for clean-up.

**Join words in a credit**, for comparing only: feat, feat., featuring, ft, ft., f., f/, with, w/, and, &, +, x, vs,
vs., versus, meets, pres., presents, a comma, a semicolon, a slash, "y", "con", "e", "et", "und". Compare by asking
whether each of the candidate's credited names appears whole in the DJ's credit; do not split the DJ's credit on
these.

**Comparing a song with a candidate.**

| Song's version | Candidate's version | Length | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| none or `plain` | none, `plain` or `remaster` | fits | The same. Choose |
| none or `plain` | `radio`, or `extended` or `club` | fits | The same record under the catalogue's word. Choose; offer the word unticked |
| none or `plain` | anything | does not fit | Another version. List |
| a kind in the Length family | the same kind, any spelling | fits | The same. Choose |
| `extended` | `club`, or the reverse | fits | Probably the same; to be checked on real rows before choosing |
| `remix`, `dub`, `vip`, `dj-edit`, `bootleg` with a name | the same kind and the same name | fits | The same. Choose |
| one of those five kinds, with a name | the same kind with another name, or with none | any | Another version. List |
| any, with lyrics clean or dirty | the same without it | fits | The same take. Choose for artist, year, label, release; the lyrics fact stays the song's |
| any Pool kind | none or `plain` | will not fit | The original. Facts offered as the original's |
| `mashup`, `transition`, `party`, `tool`, `altered`, `mixed` | anything | any | Nothing in a catalogue is this file. At most the original of its first title, as a list |
| `live`, `other-take` | not the same kind | any | Another recording. List |

**What length alone can tell.** Using the pools' own numbers:

| Observation | Reading |
|---|---|
| Within about 3 seconds of a candidate with the same title and artist | The same version, a clean or dirty twin, or a remaster. Length cannot tell these three apart, nor an instrumental from the vocal |
| Longer than the original by a steady amount at the ends | An intro or intro-and-outro edit. Eight bars last 15 seconds at 128 BPM, 19 at 100 and 21 at 90; sixteen bars twice that; both ends twice again. So roughly 15 to 85 seconds longer [AC1, AC5, AC6; arithmetic] |
| One and a half to just over two minutes | A super short edit, quick hit or snip [AC1] |
| Two and a half to three minutes, where the original is longer | A short edit [AC1] |
| About three to four minutes | Radio length; the middle radio edit in one catalogue is 3:30 [AC13] |
| Five minutes and up for a dance record | Extended or club; the middle extended mix in the same catalogue is 5:23 [AC13] |
| A minute or two shorter than the catalogue's entry, on a dance compilation | Likely a cut from a mixed release [AC22] |

These are aids for telling a DJ what a file probably is. None is a rule to fill a fact from, and under the
library-blind rule any number used must be set on music Desk has not seen.

**Where the vocabulary is uncertain.**

- **"Snip", "Hype", "Slam", "Segue", "Wordplay", "Jump Off"**: I found the words in use but no pool's own
  definition. They are grouped by guess. Nothing depends on telling them apart: each means "a pool tool, not in a
  catalogue".
- **"Edit" alone** is `radio` when a label wrote it and `dj-edit` when a DJ did. With a name it is `dj-edit`;
  without, `radio`. Length corrects a wrong guess.
- **"Mix" with a name** is read as `remix`. Some are the artist's own mixes named for a place or a mood. The name
  still has to match, so the comparison is safe either way.
- **"Club"** is kept apart from `extended` because a club mix can be a different mix. Whether a fitting length is
  enough to treat them as one should be read off the 66 trial rows AA2 mentions.
- **"Original Mix"** is `plain`, but may be the long cut or the short one. Only length says.
- **"Main" and "LP"** usually mean the dirty album cut on hip-hop promos [use]. I could not source that today, so
  they are left as not said.
- **"Radio" as clean** is a hint only.
- **Pool and editor names inside the version** ("DJcity", an editor's initials) cannot be told from a remixer's
  name without a list. Treat any left-over words as a name; a short list of pool names would let Desk say "a pool
  edit" instead. That list is not in this letter.
- **Other languages**: only the commonest Spanish and German join words are listed. Version words in other
  languages ("versión", "remezcla") are not.
- **Transition tempos**: the forms above are from use; I did not find a pool's page that sets the format.

### 5.6 Smaller points the evidence raises

- **The booth's search** (D19) looks in title and artist. A DJ also types a version word, a remixer or "clean";
  the search should look in the version and the whole credit.
- **Genre from a lookup** should never be ticked by itself over a DJ's genre, and is best shown as "the
  catalogue's".
- **A DJ's mark** could be shown in the booth as the DJ's own flag. What it means is theirs; ask working DJs
  before giving it a name.
- **A song cut from a mixed compilation** is a poor file to mix with. Desk can say so when the length and the
  release point that way; it should not hide the song.
- **The lyrics fact and the kinds are new fields**, additions to crate v1 in the manner of D27 and D34.
