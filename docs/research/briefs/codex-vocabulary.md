# Brief for Codex: the words DJs actually use for transitions (research track I)

**Product.** Deckie is a booth companion for DJs who play on gear that does not know their library. It suggests what
could come next and how to do the join, and it labels those joins in words: long blend, bass swap, echo out, filter
fade, hard cut, slam, chop, backspin, vinyl brake, loop roll, tease, and so on.

**The gap.** The research so far (docs/research/e-dj-vocabulary.md and the vocabulary page it feeds) took those words
from tutorials, manuals and vendor glossaries. Whether DJs say those words to each other, or something else entirely,
is not known, and the app should speak the way the booth speaks. A few candidate words with no source behind them
(such as "sample out" or "hard cut crossfade") are to be tested, not trusted.

**Scope.** Communities where DJs talk to each other, not vendors teaching DJs. Transitions, moves and mixing styles,
including colloquial, regional and scene-specific words (house and techno, hip-hop and open format, UK and US, mobile
and wedding, turntablists), and words for how adventurous a pick is or how far apart two tempos can be. Roughly the
last five years. Where to look and how is left to the researcher. People are quoted directly with a link to where
they said it; inference is marked apart from what was said.

**Deliverable.** The report at docs/research/codex-vocabulary.md: a one-page summary first, then one entry per move or
style with the words DJs use for it ranked by how often they were seen, who says which, and the quotes with links;
then a list of words that appear in the glossary but never in DJ speech. Read-only: no scraping, downloading or
posting.
