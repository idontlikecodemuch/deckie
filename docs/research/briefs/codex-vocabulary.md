# Brief for Codex: the words DJs actually use for transitions (research track I)

Deckie is a booth companion for DJs who play on gear that doesn't know their library. It suggests what could come
next and how to do the join, and it labels those joins in words: long blend, bass swap, echo out, filter fade, hard
cut, slam, chop, backspin, vinyl brake, loop roll, tease, and so on. My research so far (docs/research/e-dj-vocabulary.md
and the vocabulary page it feeds) took those words from tutorials, manuals and vendor glossaries. The problem: I
don't know whether DJs say those words to each other, or something else entirely, and the app should speak the way
the booth speaks. I have my own guesses, like "sample out" or "hard cut crossfade", and I don't trust them.

Scope: communities where DJs talk to each other, not vendors teaching DJs. Transitions, moves and mixing styles
are what matters, including the colloquial, regional and scene-specific words (house and techno, hip-hop and
open-format, UK and US, mobile and wedding, turntablists), and words for how adventurous a pick is or how far
apart two tempos can be. Roughly the last five years. Where you look and how is your call. Quote people directly
and link to where they said it, so I can read it myself. Say what you inferred versus what they said.

Write the report to docs/research/codex-vocabulary.md: a one-page summary first, then one entry per move or style
with the words DJs use for it ranked by how often you saw them, who says which, and the quotes with links; then a
list of words that appear in my glossary but never in DJ speech. Read-only: don't scrape, download or post.
