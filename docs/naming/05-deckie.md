Research only. Nothing was registered, no account was created, no terms were accepted, no site was logged into, nothing
was bought. Every fact is tagged **[V]** (verified by a tool named in the text, including the official register where

## 1. Summary and recommendation

**Weighted scores (out of 100): Deckie 72.2 · Deckhand 71.4 (from report 04) · Decki 64.4.**

**Deckie is a sound choice and a slight improvement on Deckhand, but it does not escape the trademark problem; it
moves it.** Deckhand's problem was an identical word registered in the US for fishing software. Deckie's problem is
**DECKEE**, a boating-safety app that is pronounced the same way. DECKEE is registered for class 9 software in the US,
Australia, the EU, the UK, New Zealand and Canada, and outside the US its registration covers "computer software,
downloadable software (apps)" in general. The US exposure is about the same as Deckhand's. The UK, EU and Australian
exposure is worse, and Australia is where the word comes from.

**Use "Deckie", not "Decki".** Decki scores 8 points lower. An iOS app is already called exactly "Decki" (a
Brazilian travel-diary app, with deckiapp.com). decki.io, decki.net and decki.co are taken. Few people would spell it
that way after hearing it. A misspelling gains nothing legally either, because examiners treat a novel spelling of a
word as that word. **Deki** is dismissed (section 4.1).

**Can the owner buy domains and handles while the attorney check runs? Yes, but only the cheap, reversible ones.**
None of the following is blocked, and together they cost well under $200 a year:

- **deckie.dj**, after a registrar confirms it is available.
- **deckie.io** and **getdeckie.com** (or **deckieapp.com**).
- The handle **deckieapp** everywhere.

**Do not buy deckie.com ($59,888 on Atom, or $2,500 down plus $2,750 a month for 24 months)**, file a trademark,
print merchandise or announce the name publicly until the attorney has looked at DECKEE.

Three sub-criteria are interpreted slightly differently here; the weights are unchanged:

- **A2 Spelling when heard:** for Deckie the issue is the ending (-ie, -y, -ee, -i), not spacing. Every spelling a

- **C1/C2:** for Deckhand, report 04 graded an identical word. Here the conflict is a mark that sounds identical and

  for the exact query, because that is what users experience.

## 4. Findings per dimension

### 4.1 Linguistics and meaning

**Pronunciation.** DECK-ee: two syllables, stress on the first, no ambiguous letters. "Decki" is mostly read the same
way, but some readers will say "DECK-eye", and Slavic readers will read *dečki* (see below). [U: general knowledge]

**Spelling when heard (the weak point).** A listener can write the word four ways, and three of them lead somewhere
else:

- **Decky**: the most natural English spelling. It leads to the Steam Deck "Decky Loader" (7,429 GitHub stars, active
  20 Oct 2026, decky.xyz) and to "decky" apps on both stores. [V: GitHub API, iTunes API, Play]
- **Deckee**: leads to the boating app.
- **Deckie**: the dictionary spelling.
- **Decki**: almost nobody writes this from hearing alone.

Green's Dictionary of Slang lists the word as "deckie"; Wiktionary also gives "decky" as a nautical form of
"deckhand". [U: search snippets of greensdictofslang.com and onelook.com] The owner must always show the word written
down: on the icon, in QR codes and on stickers in the booth. Typed searches will need the "decky" and "deckee"
misspellings handled, for example as App Store keywords.

**Length and memorability.** 6 letters (Decki 5), the same length as "Serato" and "Mixxx", and easy to remember once
seen. The "-ie" ending makes it friendly.

**Meaning to a DJ.** "Deck" is visible, and "-ie" is the friendly diminutive (as in roadie, techie, foodie). A US DJ
will read it as "the little deck helper" or "the deck sidekick" without knowing the slang. That is the right
reading, but it carries less meaning than "Deckhand". Australians and New Zealanders will get the deckhand meaning at
once, and so will British people from fishing towns (Hull's "deckie learner" is well documented in oral histories).
[V: search results for mylearning.org, merchant-navy.net, rememberingthepast.co.uk] Decki loses the "-ie" cue and
reads as a startup coinage.

**The duck reading.** Deckie/Duckie is a one-letter swap.

- deckie.app is literally a duck-themed flashcard app. [V]
- Beatport's search for "deckie" suggests the Vietnamese artist **Duckie**. [V]
- The ABN register's fuzzy search returns a dozen "Duckie" businesses. [V]
- iOS autocorrect may also turn "deckie" into "decide" or "duckie". [U: not tested on a device]

This works for the owner's sense of fun (a duck in a sailor's cap is an easy mascot), but it pushes the brand towards
"cute".

**Connotations and awkward readings.** None of these is disqualifying.

### 4.2 Fit to positioning

- **Helper, not autopilot.** A deckie is junior crew who does the work so the skipper can steer, which is exactly the
  promised relationship. The diminutive makes it even more clearly subordinate than "Deckhand". Score 5. Decki gets 4,
  because without the "-ie" outside Australia it reads as a coined word rather than a role.
- **DJ credibility.** The risk is "cute": "-ie" names can read as toy apps, and the duck association adds to that.
  This is acceptable to this audience, as the owner says, if the visual identity is grown-up: a stencil wordmark,
  dark booth UI and no mascot in the product. Score 3, against 4 for Deckhand.
- **No AI hype.** Pass.
- **App Store fields (30 characters each), measured:**
  - Name "Deckie: DJ Booth Companion" (26) + subtitle "Track ID, next track, the join" (30)
  - Name "Deckie: DJ Set Assistant" (24) + subtitle "Knows your crate. You pick." (27)
  - Name "Deckie: Crate-aware DJ helper" (29)

  "DJ" must be in the name or the subtitle, because the bare word carries no DJ meaning outside Australia and NZ.
  Apple indexes the name and subtitle, and the 100-character keyword field should include "decky" and "deckee"
  misspellings. Whether adding a competitor's mark "Deckee" as a keyword is wise is a question for the attorney; the
  safer course is to omit it. [U: Apple indexing from general knowledge]

**Likelihood of confusion with DECKEE.**

- **Sound.** The marks sound identical, DECK-ee. US, EU and UK examiners weigh this heavily, and in the EU/UK a mark
  that differs by one or two letters and sounds the same is routinely held "highly similar".
- **Sight.** The two words share five of six letters and differ only in the ending (-IE vs -EE).
- **Meaning.** Both evoke a boat deck; DECKEE's IR states the word "has no meaning", but its branding is nautical,
  which reinforces the overlap.
- **Goods, US.** The US specification is narrow (boating safety). DJ software is a different purpose, buyer and
  channel. The USPTO does not treat all software as related, so a 2(d) refusal is possible but not likely, and an
  opposition is plausible only if Deckee watches the register. Its counsel at Arnall Golden Gregory and Davies Collison
  Cave suggests it may.
- **Goods, EU/UK/AU/NZ.** "Computer software, downloadable software (apps)" covers DJ software word for word. That
  means identical goods plus sound-identical marks, so **an opposition by Deckee to a Deckie filing in these
  territories would probably succeed**, and an infringement claim on launch there is a realistic threat.
- **Possible mitigation in the EU/UK/AU** [U: legal assessment, not advice]: broad, unused terms such as "computer
  software" become vulnerable to partial revocation for non-use five years after protection (EU from about 20 Jan
  2027, UK from about 6 Jan 2027). In Australia, removal for non-use is already available. An attorney could limit
  DECKEE to boating software, or negotiate a coexistence agreement (DJ software only, never marine). That is a
  normal, often inexpensive deal between parties in different markets.

US risk is roughly equal; outside the US Deckie is worse. Hence C1 3/3/3 and C2 2/2/3.

**Registrability.** Deckie is a dictionary slang word, but it is arbitrary for DJ software (it does not describe the
goods), so it is inherently distinctive. DECKIE also appears as an element in recent Australian acceptances (THE ABLE
DECKIE, Oct 2026), which shows IP Australia does not reject the word as non-distinctive. **Decki is not stronger**:
a novel spelling of a word is treated as the word for both distinctiveness and confusion, and Decki sounds identical
to DECKEE too. [U: legal principle] C3 is 4 for both.

### 4.9 Brand and extension

**Wordmark.** A lower-case "deckie" in a sturdy stencil or condensed grotesque, to counter the cuteness. The paired
"ck" and the dotted "i" give a hook for an icon (the dot as a platter or a buoy).

**Three logo directions:**

1. **Sou'wester platter.** A fisherman's sou'wester hat seen from above, whose brim is a vinyl or jog-wheel circle.
   It reads as deckhand plus record, works in one colour at icon size, and avoids the duck.
2. **The duck on deck (embrace it).** A small, minimal duck silhouette in a sailor's cap sitting on the edge of a
   platter, for the playful version. It needs restraint (flat, two colours) to keep DJ credibility, and it moves
   visually towards deckie.app's duck, so check that first.
3. **Signal pennant i.** The "i" of deckie becomes a pitch-fader cap with a maritime pennant flying from it. It is
   abstract, typographic and scales to a favicon, and the pennant colours make a palette.

**Family.** These all read naturally:

- **Deckie** (iPad)
- **Deckie Log** (tracklists)
- **Deckie Web**
- **Deckie Learner**: a ready-made name for a practice or tutorial mode, which British DJs from fishing towns will
  smile at
- **Deckie Crate** (catalogue builder)

**Taglines:**

- "Your deckie. Your call."
- "All hands on deck. Yours on the faders."
- "Knows the track. Knows the join. Leaves the mix to you."

**In the booth.** "Deckie, what's next?" and "What's Deckie say?" carry over noise. The two syllables are clean and
the stress is on the first. It sounds like a person's nickname (as with Declan), which suits a helper.
