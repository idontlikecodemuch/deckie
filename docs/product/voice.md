# How Deckie talks

v0.1, Oct 27, 2026. A first framework for how we talk about the product and the words we use, asked for by
Christopher in his review of spec v0.6. Sources: the vocabulary research (`docs/research/e-dj-vocabulary.md`), the
two community passes (`codex-community.md`, `codex-vocabulary.md`, which quote DJs directly), and the feedback log
from the party. This is for approval and argument, not yet a style guide.

## Who we are talking to

Any DJ. In the booth they are in a hurry, in the dark, and half-listening; at home they are building a crate or a
set with time to read. The same words work in both places if they are the booth's own words. We never talk to "the
user"; we talk to a DJ.

## How Deckie sounds

Like a DJ friend who did the homework. Plain, short, specific. It knows the numbers DJs use, BPM, fader percent,
key codes, and uses no other numbers on screen. It suggests and never instructs: "could work", "try", "if you want
it", never "you should" or "wrong". It is confident about what it measured and quiet about what it guessed. No
hype, no exclamation marks, no "smart", "magic" or "AI". Sentence case. One accent colour, used only for meaning.

## The words

Use the booth's own words. The community passes found these said by DJs to each other: bring it in, mix out, mix
in, swap the bass, switch the lows, blend, long blend, cut, hard cut, chop, loop, tease, echo out, filter out,
backspin, spinback, vinyl brake, drop it on the one, open format, key lock, master tempo, half time. When Deckie
describes a pick it can also use the words DJs use for intent: keep the groove, build, let it breathe, bring the
energy up, read the room.

Avoid: "join" as the name of the thing between two tracks (it is understandable English but not a booth word; say
"transition" in prose and "mix-in point" or "mix-out point" for the places); "sync" for anything but software sync;
"not mixable" and other verdicts (say what is in the way: "tempos too far apart"); tutorial names where a booth word
exists; internal names (bass_swap_8, quick_blend_echo) anywhere a DJ can see; "track ID" for our own crate (we
"name" tracks); "AI", "smart", "intelligent", "magic".

Three registers, kept apart: plain English for anyone, the DJ's word in the booth, and how Deckie understands it.
The vocabulary page holds all three for every term. Product copy uses the DJ register; help and onboarding may
add the plain one; the Deckie register stays in our documents.

Lengths are choices, not definitions: a blend can run minutes, a bass swap can be gradual, a chop can be a beat.
Labels say the move; bar counts are a suggestion shown beside it, never part of the name.

## The promise, in one line

Candidates for the tagline and the store line, to pick from: "Know your crate." "Your crate, mixed." "Next record,
your crate." "Everything your crate can tell you." The descriptive subtitle "DJ booth companion" stays for search.

## Examples

- A pick's line: "pitch +3.5 · in key · clean 16-bar intro · blend or echo out".
- A harder pick: "pitch −9 · keys clash · cut on the one".
- Onboarding: "Pick three songs you'd play in a row. For each change, tap how you'd do it."
- Empty crate on the iPad: "No crate yet. Build one on your Mac, or add files here."
- Nothing recognised for a while: "Listening. Tap a track in your crate if it's too loud in here."
- Privacy, everywhere it is said: "Your music stays on your iPad. No account. No upload. Works with no signal."
- A learned change, on the How you mix page: "You've been pushing tempo to about 6 percent. Picks now reach that far.
  Undo."

## Open

- "Filter out" versus "filter sweep" as the label (Christopher's call; DJs say the first, Mike did not understand it).
- Whether "transition" or "mix" is the word on screen for the thing between two tracks.
- A name for the saved pair ("keeper"? "good pair"?) and for the booth screen's picks ("up next" is what DJs say).
