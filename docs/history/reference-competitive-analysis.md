`_pool build/mixer/competitive-analysis.md` (585 lines, 43 sources) analyses what was built for the Halloween party against rekordbox, Serato, VirtualDJ, djay, Traktor, Mixxx, Mixed In Key, DJ.Studio, Beatport Track ID and others, plus DJ sentiment. Headlines: no major DJ app identifies the playing track through a microphone, and ours is the only suggester that says how to do the join; the niche is decks without a network link (CDJ-1000MK3, vinyl). Auto-mix weaknesses found in the render logs: 19 of 65 joins dip >2 LU, 12 of 31 blends join clashing keys, blends are short because the vocal model over-reports, 26% of joins jump tempo >6%, and 31% of all scored pairs are labelled brake upstream. Top recommendations: listening QA per join; a key gate on blends; install Demucs (not in the mixer venv) for vocal detection and stem blends; fix level dips at the source and drop synthetic filler; plan by an energy curve with ~2% tempo steps; measure Deck recognition in a loud room and add wider pitch variants; a history strip and "why" line in Deck; line-in recording and CUE/Mixcloud export; learn from what Mike actually played; stay on development/TestFlight installs and never ship the bundled commercial catalogue. Verdict: keep as a personal tool, ~2 weeks of auto-mix fixes, then a 30-day TestFlight trial with 5-10 DJs. (The report's note that the iPad profile expires Oct 31 is wrong: the app is on Chris's own iPad under a paid account, so the profile lasts a year.)
