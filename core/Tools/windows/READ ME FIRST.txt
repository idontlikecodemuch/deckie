Deckie Desk for Windows, first test
===================================

Thank you for trying this. It is an early version, made for testing.


What this is
------------

Deckie is a DJ booth companion. Deckie Booth, on an iPad, sits beside your decks.
To do that it needs a crate file: everything Deckie knows about your songs
(tempo, bars, key, style, where the vocals are), and never the songs themselves.

Deckie Desk is the program that makes the crate file, from the music on your PC.

Your music stays on this PC. No account. No upload. It works with no internet.


How to start it
---------------

1. Unzip the whole Deckie folder first. (Right-click the zip, "Extract All".)
   It will not work from inside the zip.

2. Open the new folder and double-click "Deckie Desk".

Deckie Desk opens in a window of its own. Nothing is installed, and it does
not ask for an administrator password.

It needs Windows 10 or Windows 11, 64-bit.


"Windows protected your PC"
---------------------------

The first time you start it, Windows may show a blue box that says
"Windows protected your PC". Windows shows this for any new program it has not
seen many times before. This test version is not yet signed with a publisher's
certificate, which is what makes that box go away.

To continue: click "More info", then "Run anyway".

If you would rather not, that is fine. Tell the person who gave you Deckie.


If it says it needs "WebView2"
------------------------------

Deckie Desk's window is drawn by a free part of Windows from Microsoft, the
WebView2 Runtime. Windows 11 has it built in. On Windows 10 it arrives with
Microsoft Edge's updates, and Microsoft says the vast majority of Windows 10
PCs have it. A few do not.

If yours does not, Deckie Desk says so when you start it, and offers to open
Microsoft's own page for it:

    https://developer.microsoft.com/microsoft-edge/webview2/consumer/

Choose "Download WebView2" there, install it, and start Deckie Desk again.
Deckie downloads nothing by itself.

Or use "Make Crate File" instead. It needs nothing else. See further down.


Adding music
------------

1. Click "Add music". Pick a USB stick, an SD card or a CD from the list, or
   "Choose a folder..." for a folder on this PC.
   You can also drag a folder onto the window.

2. The window shows each song as it is read, and about how long is left.
   You can keep using the PC. It will be busy, and the fan may run.

3. When it finishes, the window says how many songs went in, how many files
   did not and why, and which songs still need a name.

Add more music the same way. Each stick, disc or folder you add keeps its own
name, its folders and the order of its songs, and they all go into one crate.

Nothing is written inside your music folders, and no song is changed, moved
or copied.


Getting the crate file to your iPad
-----------------------------------

Click "Export crate file..." and choose where it goes. You get one small file
named after your crate, such as "Friday night.deckiecrate" (about 4 KB a song).

Any of these work:

  - A USB stick or SD card: export the crate file onto it, plug it into the
    iPad, and open it from the Files app.
  - iCloud Drive: with iCloud for Windows, export the crate file into your
    iCloud Drive folder. It shows in Files on the iPad.
  - A cable: connect the iPad, open the Apple Devices app, choose the iPad,
    then Files, and drag the crate file onto Deckie Booth.
  - Email it to yourself, and open the attachment on the iPad.

Deckie Booth opens the file as it is.


How long it takes
-----------------

It reads every song from start to end, so it takes a while the first time.
These are estimates, not promises:

    a laptop from the last two or three years    about 5 seconds a song
                                                 1,000 songs in 1 to 1.5 hours
    an older laptop with 8 GB of memory          about 15 seconds a song
                                                 1,000 songs in about 4 hours

You can leave it running. If you close the window or the PC turns off, nothing
that was read is lost: start Deckie Desk again and choose "Carry on".

Adding the same music again is quick. It only reads songs that are new or
changed.


What does not work yet
----------------------

  - Song recognition. Deckie Booth names the song that is playing by a sound
    fingerprint of each song, kept in the crate file. When this page was
    written (Nov 14, 2026), Deckie Desk for Windows did not write one yet, so
    with a crate file made on a PC you pick the song in the booth yourself.
    It is being built. Your version tells you which it is: if the line under
    your songs says some "can't be recognised yet", those have no fingerprint.

  - Key, on some songs. Deckie reads each song's key itself, and takes a key
    your files or your DJ program already hold. Where Deckie is sure, it shows
    the key. Where it found the home note but not whether the song is major or
    minor, it shows the home note. Where it is not sure, it shows no key and
    tells you how many songs that is. It does not guess.

  - Looking up names. A song with no name in its file is listed as needing
    one. You can type it, paste a list, or take names from the file names or a
    playlist. Nothing is looked up on the internet in this version.

  - This test has not been tried on many PCs yet. Reading a music CD, and
    dragging a folder onto the window, are the two things least tried.

File types it reads:    MP3, FLAC, WAV, AIFF, and M4A (AAC and Apple Lossless,
                        such as iTunes purchases)
Not yet:                WMA, Ogg and Opus
Never:                  copy-protected files (old iTunes Store purchases,
                        Apple Music downloads). Only Apple's own apps play those.

A few M4A files do not say exactly where their music starts. Deckie still reads
them: their beats may sit a touch late (up to 0.05 seconds).

Files it cannot read are counted and listed. Nothing is skipped without telling
you. Deckie does not mix, and it does not change your music.


"Make Crate File", the other way
--------------------------------

The same folder holds a second program, "Make Crate File". It has no window of
its own: drag your music folder onto it, and it reads the folder and writes the
crate file next to it ("My Music.deckiecrate" for a folder called "My Music"),
with a short log beside it. It needs nothing from Windows but Windows.

Use it if Deckie Desk will not open on your PC, or if you prefer it.
It makes a crate file from one folder. Deckie Desk does more: several sticks
and folders in one crate, names, and listening to a song to name it.


If something goes wrong
-----------------------

Please tell the person who gave you Deckie:

  1. What you clicked, and what the window said. A photo or screenshot helps.
  2. Which Windows you have (Windows 10 or 11).

If Deckie Desk does not open at all and says nothing, say that too. It is the
most useful thing we can learn from this test.

To remove Deckie: delete this folder. It installed nothing. It keeps your
crates, and its notes on what it has already read, in two folders called
"Deckie Desk" and "Deckie" under your Windows account's app data
(type %LOCALAPPDATA% into the address bar of a folder window), and the
window's own small storage in a folder called "deckie-desk.exe" under
%APPDATA%. You can delete those too.
