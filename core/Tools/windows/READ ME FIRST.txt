Deckie for PC, first test
=========================

Thank you for trying this. It is an early version, made for testing.


What this is
------------

Deckie is a DJ booth companion. Deckie Booth, on an iPad, sits beside your decks.
To do that it needs a crate file: everything Deckie knows about your songs
(tempo, bars, style, where the vocals are), and never the songs themselves.

This program makes that crate file from a folder of music on your PC.

Your music stays on this PC. No account. No upload. It works with no internet.


How to use it
-------------

1. Unzip the whole Deckie folder first. (Right-click the zip, "Extract All".)
   It will not work from inside the zip.

2. Drag your music folder onto "Make Crate File".
   Or double-click "Make Crate File" and it will ask for the folder.

3. A window opens and shows each song as it is read, and about how long is left.
   You can keep using the PC. It will be busy, and the fan may run.

4. When it finishes, the window says how many songs went in, how many did not
   and why, and where the crate file is. Press Enter to close it.

The crate file appears next to your music folder, with the folder's name.
For a folder called "My Music" you get:

    My Music.deckiecrate        the crate file
    My Music Deckie log.txt     a short log of the run

Nothing is written inside your music folder, and no song is changed.


"Windows protected your PC"
---------------------------

The first time you start it, Windows may show a blue box that says
"Windows protected your PC". Windows shows this for any new program it has not
seen many times before. This test version is not yet signed with a publisher's
certificate, which is what makes that box go away.

To continue: click "More info", then "Run anyway".

If you would rather not, that is fine. Tell the person who gave you Deckie.


How long it takes
-----------------

It reads every song from start to end, so it takes a while the first time.
These are estimates, not promises:

    a laptop from the last two or three years    about 5 seconds a song
                                                 1,000 songs in 1 to 1.5 hours
    an older laptop with 8 GB of memory          about 15 seconds a song
                                                 1,000 songs in about 4 hours

You can leave it running. If you close the window or the PC turns off, nothing
is lost: start it again with the same folder and it carries on where it stopped.

The second time is quick. It only reads songs that are new or changed.


What it does not do yet
-----------------------

A crate file made on a PC is not complete yet. Two things are missing:

  - Song recognition. Deckie Booth will not yet name the song that is playing
    from a crate file made on a PC. You pick the song yourself.
  - Key. A crate file made on a PC has no key yet, unless your files already
    carry one.

Both are being worked on. Tempo, bars, style and vocals are all there.

File types it reads:    MP3, FLAC, WAV, AIFF
Not yet:                M4A and AAC (iTunes purchases, Apple Lossless), WMA,
                        Ogg and Opus
Never:                  copy-protected files (old iTunes Store purchases,
                        Apple Music downloads). Only Apple's own apps play those.

Files it cannot read are counted and listed at the end. Nothing is skipped
without telling you.

It does not read audio CDs. It does not mix, and it does not change, move or
copy your music.


Getting the crate file to your iPad
-----------------------------------

The crate file is one small file (about 4 KB a song). Any of these work:

  - Email it to yourself, and open the attachment on the iPad.
  - Put it in iCloud Drive, OneDrive, Google Drive or Dropbox on the PC, and
    open it from the Files app on the iPad.
  - Plug the iPad into the PC with its cable, open iTunes or the Apple Devices
    app, and drop it into Deckie Booth's files.

Opening a PC-made crate file needs a version of Deckie Booth that knows this
one-file form. The person who gave you this will tell you when yours does.


If something goes wrong
-----------------------

Please send two things to the person who gave you Deckie:

  1. The log file next to your music folder ("... Deckie log.txt").
  2. A photo or screenshot of the window, if it shows a message.

What is in the log: the version of the program, what kind of PC this is
(Windows version, processor count, memory), how many songs were found and how
long they took, and the files that could not be added, each with its name and
the reason. That is all. No song audio, nothing about the songs that went in,
and not the name of your Windows account.

To remove Deckie: delete this folder. It installed nothing. It keeps its notes
on what it has already read in a folder called "Deckie" under your Windows
account's local app data, which you can delete too.
